\paragraph{Queen and pawn against queen}\label{queen-and-pawn}\index{Queen endings!Queen and pawn against queen}
Queen endings are difficult. Almost every move is a check, and it is hard to see whether the checks
will ever stop. Computers have shown that a win, when there is one, can take more than fifty moves.
We will not show such long lines. Instead, here are the general principles that Dvoretsky gives
for queen and pawn against queen~\cite{Dvoretsky:2008-endgame-manual}. They are enough to judge
most positions and to find the right plan.

How to judge the position.
\begin{itemize}
\item If the defender's king stands in front of the pawn, the draw is usually easy: he can even
offer the exchange of queens.
\item If the defender's king is far away, the result depends on the pawn. With a central pawn or a
bishop's pawn, practically every such position is lost: the defender can only hope for a mistake,
or try to bring his king in front of the pawn. With a knight's pawn, the attacker usually wins,
but there are drawn positions. With a rook's pawn, most positions are drawn, although the defence
is still hard.
\item The draws with a knight's or rook's pawn come from a drawing zone: the corner
diagonally opposite the pawn's queening square (see below). Against a central or bishop's pawn
there is no such zone.\index{Queen endings!Drawing zone}
\item The further the pawn has advanced, the smaller the defender's chances.
\end{itemize}

How to defend. Keep your queen in the centre. Against a knight's or rook's pawn, put your
king near the corner diagonally opposite the pawn's queening square: for a g-pawn, near a1. There
it is hardest for the attacker to answer a check with a check.

How to attack. Keep your queen in the centre too; the closer it is to the edge, the harder
the win and the greater the danger of perpetual check. Do not hide your king in front of your own
pawn. Instead, bring it to the same file or rank as the enemy king, or to the next one. Then many
checks can be answered by putting the queen in between with a check of your own, and the checks
come to an end.\index{Queen endings!Counter-check}
