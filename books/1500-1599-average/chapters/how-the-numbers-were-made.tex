\section{Words used in this book}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.6cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{11.4cm}@{}}
\textbf{Endgame} & A position where each side has at most the number of pieces in the endgame rule (queens, rooks, bishops, knights; kings and pawns not counted). \\[4pt]
\textbf{Material} & The pieces each side has, whoever has which: “Rook and bishop vs rook and knight” counts both colours. A game counts once for each material it reaches. \\[4pt]
\textbf{Exchanges} & A material counts only once it stands for at least 2 moves (one by each side). Shorter states in the middle of an exchange belong to the material before them. \\[4pt]
\textbf{Winning} & At least +1.50 for one side in Stockfish's score; below that the position counts as equal. \\[4pt]
\textbf{Blunder} & A move after which the mover drops from winning to equal, from equal to losing, or from winning to losing, while the engine's best move was a different move and at least one move kept the result. Marked moves that fail this test are engine noise near the 1.50 line, not mistakes. \\[4pt]
\textbf{Per 100 games that reach it} & Blunders made in this material, per 100 games that reached it (\xref{chapter}{ch:go-wrong}). \\[4pt]
\textbf{Per 100 games played} & Blunders made in this material per 100 games in the data, whether or not they reached it: how often it happens times how often it goes wrong (\xref{chapter}{ch:where-to-start}). \\[4pt]
\textbf{Ranking} & Only materials reached in at least 500 games are ranked in \xref{chapter}{ch:go-wrong}. With fewer games, splitting the data in two halves gave a different top of the list. \\[4pt]
\end{tabular}
