\paragraph{Good placement}\index{Rook endings!Good placement of the defender}

Put together, the defender's pieces have their places. The king belongs on the
\term{short-side}; the rook belongs on the \term{long-side}, on the first or the eighth rank. From
there it is ready for \term{side-checks} as well as for \term{rear-checks}. Without a concrete
reason, do not improvise: put the pieces there, and keep them there.

And whenever you defend, first check whether one of these resources gives you a draw at once.
If it does, use it: a \term{clear-cut-draw} is worth more than a better position that still
has to be held.\index{Practical play!Clear-cut draw}
